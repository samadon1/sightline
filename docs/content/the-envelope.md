# The Envelope

Final scene for the Fire TV caption prototype and the user study. It combines the strongest beats from the two micro-scenes: a fast exchange with one speaker partly turned away, a voice from off screen whose identity is concealed, one real overlap, one glass break off screen to the right, and a reveal. All of it comes from ordinary domestic behaviour. The scene has to work as a small piece of drama first; the caption test rides on top of it.

- Running time: 53 to 55 seconds
- Location: one kitchen
- Characters: Maya (mainly left), Daniel (mainly right), Amara (brief, enters at 30 s), UNKNOWN VOICE (off screen, revealed as Amara)
- Dialogue only. No music. No visible third-party trademarks. Original script.
- Deliverable: 1080p H.264 / AAC master plus an HLS rendition with fMP4 segments

## Logline

A handwritten envelope with no name on it lands on the kitchen table. Maya and Daniel each decide it is theirs. A voice from the hallway tells them to put it down, a jug breaks, and the real owner walks in.

## Blocking notes

- Maya lives on frame left at the counter. Her natural task (sorting post, putting letters on a shelf) keeps her turned away for roughly 0 to 9 s and again for the last five seconds. She faces Daniel from "Not until we know" until Amara leaves.
- Daniel lives on frame right. He starts seated at the table and moves to the counter at 11.5 s, but he approaches from the right and stops on Maya's right, so he never crosses her.
- The hallway door is on the right edge of frame, slightly ajar. The performer playing Amara delivers the UNKNOWN VOICE lines from behind it at normal volume. No disguised voice.
- The overlap at 23.8 to 25.4 s is Maya first, Daniel about a third of a second behind. Both lines must remain intelligible; rehearse until they are.
- The glass break is performed live by an assistant behind the door on the right: a glass jar dropped into a metal bucket with a towel in the bottom. Safety glasses, sweep between takes. Also record it as a wild track after the last take for the sound edit.
- Amara enters from the right, stays right of Daniel, and exits the same way. She is on screen for about fifteen seconds in total.
- Nobody crosses the room at any point. Left is Maya's side. Right is Daniel's side and the door.

## Fair Standard captions

Rules applied: Netflix English Timed Text Style Guide (42 characters per line, two lines, up to 20 cps, IDs and sound effects lowercase in brackets, IDs only where the speaker cannot be identified visually, hyphen without space for two speakers in one caption, bottom-heavy line breaks) plus the general Netflix timing floor of 5/6 second per cue and a gap of at least two frames between cues. All cues below were checked against those limits.

Two speaker IDs are used, both for the off-screen voice before the reveal. Everyone else is on screen when they speak, so no other IDs appear. Direction of the glass break is not in the text; a Standard track does not carry it.

## Production plan

### Location

One real kitchen with a counter along one wall, a small table, and a doorway to a hallway that can be placed on the right of frame. A living room with a kitchen visible would also work, but a kitchen is better: sorting post at a counter gives Maya a reason to turn away, and a jug in the hallway gives us the break. Choose a room where the window is behind or beside the camera, not behind the performers.

Check before the day: no branded appliances facing camera (or tape over badges), no printed art, calendars or posters in frame, no clock with a maker's name, fridge free of magnets and notes, no visible packaging.

### Lighting (practicals only)

- Key: daylight from a window on the camera side of the room. Shoot mid-morning or early afternoon so it stays steady for two hours. Net curtain or a bedsheet over the window if it is harsh.
- Fill: the room's ceiling light, switched on.
- Accent: a table lamp or under-cabinet light near Daniel's side so both sides of frame carry similar exposure. If the counter is dark, a second lamp on the counter, out of shot.
- Match colour temperature. Use bulbs of the same type in every practical (all warm white or all daylight). Mixed bulbs give one side an orange cast and the other a blue cast.
- Lock exposure and white balance on the camera or phone once it looks right. Do not use auto.

### Audio

- Preferred: two wired or wireless lavalier mics, one on Maya and one on Daniel, into a small two-channel recorder. Amara shares Daniel's mic space on the right; if a third lav is available, use it. Off-screen lines from behind the door are picked up by the room and the right-side lav, which is what we want.
- Alternative: a single shotgun mic on a boom or stand, just above frame, pointed between the two main positions.
- Backup: a phone running a voice recorder app, placed on the counter between the two performers, out of shot. Start it at the top of every take.
- Record 30 seconds of room tone at the end, everyone silent, mics live.
- Record a wild track of the glass break, three drops, after the last take.
- Slate every take by voice ("Envelope, setup one, take three") and a single hand clap in front of the camera, so the phone backup and the main recorder can be lined up.

### Two-hour shooting schedule

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | Load in. Set camera at setup 1. Clear trademarks and prints from frame. Hang window diffusion. Switch on practicals. Mic up. Level check. |
| 0:15 to 0:25 | Rehearse the scene twice at full speed with the door and the glass. Fix the overlap timing. Confirm nobody crosses. |
| 0:25 to 0:55 | Setup 1 (master). Four takes. Sweep glass between takes. |
| 0:55 to 1:20 | Setup 2 (tighter). Three takes. |
| 1:20 to 1:35 | Setup 3 (envelope inserts). Then the glass break wild track, three drops. |
| 1:35 to 1:45 | Room tone. Any wild lines that were stepped on. |
| 1:45 to 2:00 | Signatures on releases if not already done. Back up all media to two drives. Restore the room. |

If running late, drop setup 3 first, then reduce setup 2 to two takes. Never skip room tone.

### Export spec

Master: 1920x1080, H.264 High profile, level 4.1, 30 fps constant, yuv420p, AAC-LC stereo 48 kHz.

Encode the master from the edit export (a high-bitrate intermediate such as ProRes or a high-bitrate H.264):

```
ffmpeg -i the-envelope-edit.mov \
  -vf "scale=1920:1080:flags=lanczos,fps=30,format=yuv420p" \
  -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -level 4.1 -preset slow -crf 18 \
  -x264-params "keyint=60:min-keyint=60:scenecut=0" \
  -c:a aac -ar 48000 -ac 2 -b:a 192k \
  -movflags +faststart \
  the-envelope-1080p.mp4
```

HLS rendition with fMP4 segments (single 1080p variant plus a 720p fallback, four-second segments, keyframe on every segment boundary):

```
ffmpeg -i the-envelope-1080p.mp4 \
  -filter_complex "[0:v]split=2[v1][v2];[v1]scale=1920:1080[v1080];[v2]scale=1280:720[v720]" \
  -map "[v1080]" -map 0:a -map "[v720]" -map 0:a \
  -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -level 4.1 -preset slow -r 30 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -g 120 -keyint_min 120 -sc_threshold 0 \
  -b:v:0 5000k -maxrate:v:0 5350k -bufsize:v:0 7500k \
  -b:v:1 2800k -maxrate:v:1 2996k -bufsize:v:1 4200k \
  -c:a aac -ar 48000 -ac 2 -b:a 192k \
  -f hls -hls_time 4 -hls_playlist_type vod \
  -hls_segment_type fmp4 -hls_flags independent_segments \
  -hls_fmp4_init_filename "init_%v.mp4" \
  -hls_segment_filename "hls/seg_%v_%03d.m4s" \
  -var_stream_map "v:0,a:0,name:1080p v:1,a:1,name:720p" \
  -master_pl_name master.m3u8 \
  "hls/stream_%v.m3u8"
```

Notes:

- `-g 120` at 30 fps gives a keyframe every four seconds, which lines up with `-hls_time 4`.
- The prototype renders the approved WebVTT track itself, so the HLS playlists do not need an embedded subtitle rendition. If a player-native track is wanted for comparison, add an `#EXT-X-MEDIA:TYPE=SUBTITLES` entry that points at a WebVTT playlist, but keep the study builds on the app's own renderer so all three modes share one code path.
- Verify with `ffprobe -show_streams the-envelope-1080p.mp4` that the video is `h264`, profile `High`, `1920x1080`, `30/1` frame rate, and the audio is `aac` at `48000` Hz.

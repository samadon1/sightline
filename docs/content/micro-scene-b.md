# Micro-scene B: "Hallway"

Rough phone-shot test scene. Purpose: an off-screen voice, one short two-speaker overlap, one narratively meaningful directional sound (glass breaking off screen to the right), and one concealed identity that is revealed only after the relevant cue. Nothing here is engineered against Standard captions. Voices from the next room, people talking at once, and something breaking out of view are everyday events.

- Running time: about 24 seconds
- Location: living room (or the kitchen, shot toward the hallway door)
- Performers: Maya (left), Daniel (right), Amara (off screen until 15.5 s, then brief)
- Camera: one phone, two setups
- Audio: clean dialogue, no music; the glass break is performed live off screen right, plus a wild track for safety

## Logline

A voice from the hallway wants help with the door. Maya and Daniel both volunteer at once, neither moves, and something breaks. The voice turns out to be their housemate Amara, who has been home longer than they thought.

## Blocking notes

- Maya sits on the sofa on frame left, body angled toward Daniel and the hallway door.
- Daniel sits in an armchair or on the sofa arm on frame right, nearest the hallway door.
- The hallway door is on the right edge of frame, slightly ajar. The unknown voice comes from behind it. The performer playing Amara stands in the hallway, out of sight, and speaks at normal volume toward the door. Do not put on a voice. The concealment comes from the door, not from acting.
- The overlap: Maya says "I'll go" and Daniel says "I've got it" starting about a third of a second later. Both start to stand, then both stop. Rehearse this twice so the overlap is real but the words are still audible.
- The glass break: an assistant in the hallway drops a glass jar into a metal bucket lined at the bottom with a towel to catch the pieces. Safety glasses for the assistant, sweep after each take. It must happen on the right, behind the door, never in shot.
- Amara enters from the right and stays right of Daniel. Nobody crosses.

## Shot list (one phone, two setups)

| Setup | Framing | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| 2 | Same axis, a little tighter on the two seated performers, door still visible at the right edge | Used for the overlap and the reaction to the glass. Two takes. |

Keep the door in frame in both setups so the viewer can see where the voice and the break come from, but never see who is behind it until Amara steps through.

## Props

- A plain fabric shopping bag with a damp patch on the bottom (for Amara's entrance)
- A glass jar and a metal bucket with a towel in the bottom (for the break, off screen)
- Safety glasses and a dustpan (crew)
- A hallway door that can sit ajar
- Sofa and chair with plain cushions, no printed art or posters in frame

## Fair Standard captions

Speaker IDs appear only for the off-screen voice, because that speaker cannot be seen. Since the identity is not known to the viewer at that point, the ID is a neutral descriptor. The Netflix guide gives [man] and [woman] as examples of generic IDs; we use [unknown voice] instead because a gendered descriptor would give away the reveal in a three-person cast. That choice is still within the guide's rule: lowercase, in brackets, descriptive, used only because the speaker is not visible.

The glass break is captioned as a lowercase bracketed sound effect. Standard captions do not carry direction, so the text is simply [glass shatters]. The direction lives in the companion metadata.

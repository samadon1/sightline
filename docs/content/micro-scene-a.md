# Micro-scene A: "Keys"

Rough phone-shot test scene. Purpose: a rapid, visible exchange between three speakers with natural blocking, where one speaker spends part of the scene facing away. Nothing in the scene is designed to defeat Standard captions. The ambiguity is the ordinary kind: fast turns, a back turned, two people talking in the same beat.

- Running time: about 23 seconds
- Location: kitchen (same kitchen as "The Envelope")
- Performers: Maya (left), Daniel (right), Amara (brief, enters late)
- Camera: one phone, two setups
- Audio: clean dialogue only, no music

## Logline

Two housemates tear the kitchen apart looking for car keys while a taxi waits. The third housemate walks in with the answer, and it is not one anyone wanted.

## Blocking notes

- Maya is at the counter on frame left. For the first nine seconds she faces the counter and cupboards, so the camera sees her in profile or from behind. She turns to face Daniel only at "Check it a third time." This is normal behaviour for someone searching; do not exaggerate it.
- Daniel sits at the table on frame right, facing roughly toward Maya (camera left). He stands at "I've checked my jacket twice" and stays on the right side of frame.
- Amara enters through the back doorway, which sits in the centre or slightly right of the background. She stops in the middle ground so she does not cross in front of either speaker.
- Nobody crosses the room. Left stays left and right stays right for the whole scene.
- Maya and Daniel speak on top of each other's last syllable during the "hook / jacket" exchange. It should feel like a real bicker, not a drill.

## Shot list (one phone, two setups)

| Setup | Framing | Notes |

| 2 | Same axis, one step forward and slightly tighter | Same left/right positions. Used for a second pass of the scene so the edit can cut on the fast exchange if needed. Shoot two takes. |

Both setups stay on the same side of the line between Maya and Daniel, so screen direction never flips.

## Props

- A drawer with ordinary kitchen bits (plain tea towels, wooden spoons, no branded packaging)
- A set of keys on a plain blue key tag (unbranded)
- A jacket on the back of Daniel's chair, no logos
- A plain mug for Daniel
- A fridge in the background (tape over or turn away any maker's badge)

## Fair Standard captions

Authored as a professional captioner would author them under the Netflix English Timed Text Style Guide: speaker IDs only where the speaker cannot be identified visually, sound effects in lowercase brackets, no more than 42 characters per line, no more than two lines, reading speed at or under 20 characters per second, hyphen without a space for two speakers in one caption only where the cueing genuinely calls for it.

In this scene every speaker is on screen when they speak, so a captioner would use no speaker IDs at all. That is the fair baseline.

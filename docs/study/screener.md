# Screener: TV caption prototype study

Use as a Google Form. Keep the intro to the three lines below. Estimated time to complete: 3 minutes.

**Intro text:** We are testing a new way of showing captions on a TV. This is a product test, not medical research. Sessions are 45 to 60 minutes over a captioned video call (or written chat), and we pay 60 USD (35 USD for a shorter pilot session). Please answer these questions so we can see if the study fits you.

## Questions

1. **How would you describe your hearing?** (choose one, or write your own)
   - Deaf
   - deaf or deafened (lost hearing later in life)
   - Hard of hearing
   - Hearing aid user
   - Cochlear implant user
   - Other: ________

2. **Do you use a sign language?** (choose one)
   - Yes, it is my main language (which one? ________)
   - Yes, sometimes
   - No

3. **How often do you watch TV shows or films with captions or subtitles on?**
   - Every day
   - A few times a week
   - About once a week
   - Less than once a week

4. **What do you mostly watch with captions?** (tick all that apply)
   - Dramas, series, films (stories with characters)
   - News, sport, documentaries
   - YouTube or short videos
   - Other: ________

5. **Where do you usually watch with captions?** (tick all that apply)
   - A TV in a living room or bedroom
   - A laptop or desktop
   - A phone or tablet

6. **Which caption settings do you change from the default?** (tick all that apply)
   - Text size (bigger or smaller)
   - Font or colour
   - Background box or opacity
   - Position on screen
   - None, I use the default
   - Not sure

7. **Have you used captions that show who is speaking, for example a name before the line or colours for different people?**
   - Yes, often
   - Yes, a few times
   - No
   - Not sure
   (Optional: what did you think of them? ________)

8. **Do you have a TV you can use for the session?** (choose the best match)
   - Yes, a TV with a Fire TV Stick or Fire TV built in
   - Yes, a TV I can cast to from my phone or laptop (Chromecast, AirPlay, screen mirroring)
   - Yes, a TV I can connect a laptop to with an HDMI cable
   - No TV available; I could watch on a laptop or monitor
   - I could come to an in-person session in Accra, Ghana

9. **Which session format do you prefer?** (choose one)
   - Video call with a sign language interpreter (which language? ASL, BSL, GSL, Auslan, other: ________)
   - Video call with live captions, no interpreter
   - Written chat only (no camera or voice needed)
   - Other: ________

10. **When are you usually free for a 45 to 60 minute session between Sep 9 and Sep 26?** Please give your city or time zone and two or three good windows. ________

11. **How old are you?** (choose one)
    - 18 to 24
    - 25 to 34
    - 35 to 49
    - 50 to 64
    - 65 or older

12. **Consent to quote.** We may use short anonymous quotes from your session (no name, no location, no video). Is that OK?
    - Yes
    - Yes, but show me the quote first
    - No

**Contact details:** name or nickname, email, and preferred messaging app (WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, SMS). Preferred payment method: local Amazon gift card, PayPal, mobile money (MTN, M-Pesa), bank transfer, other.

## Inclusion criteria

A person qualifies when all of these are true:

- Age 18 or older.
- Answers Q1 as Deaf, deaf or deafened, hard of hearing, hearing aid user, or cochlear implant user.
- Watches captioned content at least a few times a week (Q3), and ticks dramas, series, or films in Q4. Daily viewers are prioritised.
- Can join a session in one of the offered formats (Q9).
- Has a TV path (Q8, any of the first three options or the in-person option). For the pilot only, a laptop or monitor is acceptable if it gets us to three confirmed by Sep 12; mark those sessions as non-TV in the scoring sheet.
- Can be paid by one of the listed methods.

Exclusion: anyone who works on caption rendering products or has seen this prototype before. Ask this at booking, not on the form.

Order of priority when there are more volunteers than slots: daily narrative viewers with a TV path first, then quota gaps, then earliest availability.

# Recruitment plan: DHH caption study (pilot Sep 9 to 14, final by Sep 27)

Prepared 2026-09-02. All outreach is done by Samuel from his own accounts. Nothing in this document has been sent to anyone. Where a posting rule could not be verified from a public page, it says so.

Targets: four confirmed participants by Mon Sep 8, hard gate of three confirmed by Fri Sep 12, final panel of 5 to 8 by Sep 27 (at least two Deaf sign-language users, at least two hard of hearing, at least two who watch captioned narrative TV daily, varied ages). Ghana is one channel among many, not the target population.

## A. Channels

Expected yield is a judgement, not a measurement. It means completed screeners that turn into a booked session, from one post or one email, within about a week.

### Community forums and chat

1. **r/deaf** (about 49k members). URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/deaf/
   What it is: the largest general Deaf and hard of hearing subreddit, mixed Deaf, HoH, late-deafened, and CODA members, mostly US and UK.
   Rules found: secondary sources say the sub requires pre-approval by modmail for all research, survey, and study posts, and one summary says the mods reserve the right to remove any request for research participants. I could not load the rules page directly, so read the sidebar before posting. Sources: [gummysearch r/deaf stats](https://gummysearch.com/r/deaf/), [Grokipedia r/deaf summary](https://grokipedia.com/page/rdeaf).
   Contact path: send modmail first with the screener link, compensation, and a one-line description. Post only after approval. Use the forum post from outreach-messages.md.
   Expected yield: 1 to 3 if approved. Good source for daily caption users and for HoH diversity.

2. **r/hardofhearing**. URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/hardofhearing/
   What it is: smaller sub focused on HoH and hearing-aid and cochlear-implant users, many of whom watch TV with captions every day.
   Rules found: not verified. I could not load the rules page. Assume research posts need mod approval and message the mods first.
   Contact path: modmail, then post.
   Expected yield: 1 to 2.

3. **Deaf & Hard of Hearing Community (and Allies) Discord**. Listing: https://top.gg/servers/844362337107574823
   What it is: public D/HoH server with video signing rooms and support channels, phone-verified accounts only.
   Rules found: server has anti-spam rules and requires phone verification (from the listing). Research-post rules not verified. Ask a moderator in the intro or help channel before posting anything.
   Expected yield: 0 to 2. Young skew.

4. **Deaf International Discord**. Listing: https://top.gg/discord/servers/722573650801233920
   What it is: server open to Deaf, HoH, and CODA people from many countries, which fits the international brief.
   Rules found: not verified. Ask a mod first.
   Expected yield: 0 to 2. Useful for non-US time zones.

5. **CCAC, Collaborative for Communication Access via Captioning**. URL: https://ccacaptioning.org/join/
   What it is: volunteer captioning-advocacy community, US registered nonprofit with international members, many of them deaf, deafened, or hard of hearing captioning users. Membership is a suggested donation of 25 USD for consumers, with a waiver available on request.
   Rules found: no public posting rule seen. It runs as an email community, so requests go through the organizers.
   Contact path: join or email the organizers with the organization email from outreach-messages.md and ask whether they will circulate the call.
   Expected yield: 1 to 3, and these are exactly the habitual caption users the study needs.

## C. Compensation guidance

Benchmarks for remote moderated sessions, all in USD:

- User Interviews 2025 incentives report, drawn from about 20,000 projects: consumer 1:1 remote interviews about 80 USD per hour, professional about 85 USD per hour. Source: https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/research-incentives-report
- Respondent suggests 90 to 200 USD per hour for general-population studies and reports an average project incentive of 95 USD. Source: https://help.respondent.io/en/articles/5471087-determining-the-right-incentive-for-research-participants
- Tremendous survey work found about 57 USD for a 30-minute online interview and about 69 USD for 60 minutes. Source: https://www.tremendous.com/blog/how-much-research-incentives-pay-participants/
- Nielsen Norman Group reports an average of 64 USD per hour of test time for external usability participants, and notes that word-of-mouth accessibility recruiting through community leaders cost less than half of an agency quote while still paying participants well. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recruiting-test-participants-for-usability-studies/
- Prolific floor for unmoderated work: 8 USD per hour minimum, 12 USD recommended. This is a floor for surveys, not a target for moderated sessions. Source: https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445230-prolific-s-payment-principles

Recommendation for this study:

- Pilot session (about 30 minutes): 35 USD.
- Final session (45 to 60 minutes): 60 USD. This sits in the 60 to 80 USD per hour band above.
- Add 15 USD when a remote participant sets up TV casting or an HDMI link before the session, because that is real work.
- Pay everyone the same headline amount regardless of country. Local purchasing power differs, but paying less to some people for the same session is hard to justify and hard to explain.
- Interpreters are paid separately at their normal rate and are never counted as participant compensation.
- Say the amount in every post. Pay within 48 hours of the session.

Gift cards across countries:

- Amazon gift cards are locked to one storefront (Amazon.com cannot be redeemed on Amazon.co.uk or Amazon.ca). Send the card for the participant's own store. Source: https://www.tremendous.com/blog/international-amazon-gift-cards/
- Tremendous offers Amazon cards in 39 countries and other rewards in over 200 countries, but not in a few sanctioned countries. Source: same page and https://www.tremendous.com/catalog/
- Ghana: Amazon has no local storefront and PayPal receiving is limited, so use MTN Mobile Money or a bank transfer. Kenya: M-Pesa. Nigeria: bank transfer. UK: Amazon.co.uk card or bank transfer. US and Canada: Amazon card for that country, or PayPal. Australia: Amazon.com.au or PayPal. India: Amazon.in or UPI.
- Ask the participant's preferred method on the screener so nobody is paid in something they cannot use.

## D. Accessibility of the outreach itself

- Plain language everywhere. Short sentences, no jargon, one idea per paragraph. State the compensation, the time, and what the person will do in the first three lines.
- Offer a signed version. Record a 60-second GSL or ASL version of the call where possible, or ask a Deaf contact to sign it. A signed video on Facebook or in a Discord post reaches people a text post does not.
- Session language and interpreters. Offer ASL, BSL, GSL, or another sign language through a booked interpreter, or a written-chat session over Zoom chat or Google Docs. Let the participant choose. Ask about interpreter preference on the screener and book at least three days ahead.
- Video relay. Participants in the US can join by phone through VRS if they prefer; in the UK, Relay UK. Do not assume; ask.
- Captioned calls. Zoom automated captions are available on free accounts once the host enables them ([Zoom announcement](https://www.zoom.com/en/blog/zoom-auto-generated-captions/)). Google Meet live captions are free on personal accounts; translated captions are not ([Google Meet help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/15077804)). Turn captions on before the participant joins, and say in the invite that captions will be on.
- Automatic captions are not a substitute for an interpreter or a human captioner when a Deaf participant asks for one.
- Time zones. Put every session time in the participant's own zone and in UTC. Offer at least three slots across morning, afternoon, and evening in their zone. From Ghana (UTC), Australia and India fit early morning, the Americas fit evening.
- Make it easy to say no. One line saying the person can stop at any time and still be paid for time spent.

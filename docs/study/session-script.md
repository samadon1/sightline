# Moderator script: 45-minute caption prototype session

Conditions: **A = Standard** (conventional bottom captions, professionally authored), **B = Speaker-aware** (verified speaker label on the first cue of a run, stable left/right/bottom lane), **C = Detailed** (adds restrained speaker colour and one directional sound treatment; reduced motion on). Do not use these names with participants. Call them Style 1, 2, and 3 in the order they see them.

Pilot (Sep 9 to 14): two or three phone-shot micro-scenes of 15 to 25 seconds each, each rendered in all three styles. Final (by Sep 27): one polished 45 to 60 second scene in all three styles. Both use the same script below; in the pilot, repeat the per-condition block for each micro-scene and keep the questions to the ones that apply to that scene.

## Timing

| Block | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Welcome, interpreter check, consent | 5 |
| Calibration | 4 |
| Condition 1 (clip, questions, ratings, open questions) | 9 |
| Condition 2 | 9 |
| Condition 3 | 9 |
| Preference ranking and final questions | 5 |
| Close and payment | 4 |
| Total | 45 |

## 1. Welcome and interpreter check (5 min)

Say: "Thanks for joining. Before we start, can you see me and the captions clearly? [If an interpreter is present] Can you see the interpreter well? Interpreter, can you see and hear me?"

If written chat: "Reply OK when you can see this. I will type everything, and you can take as long as you need."

Confirm consent (consent-and-info-sheet.md). Ask about recording and quotes. Note the answers on the scoring sheet header.

Say: "We are testing three caption styles on the same scene. There are no right answers. If anything is hard to read, tell me, that is useful."

## 2. Calibration (4 min)

- "Please sit where you normally sit to watch TV." Note the approximate distance (ask them to estimate in steps or metres).
- Play the calibration card (a still frame with a two-line caption at the prototype's default size).
- "Can you read that comfortably from where you sit?" If no, step size up one notch and repeat. Record the size used. Use the same size for all three conditions.
- "Is that the size you normally use at home?" Record yes, larger, or smaller.
- For remote participants: confirm they are watching on the TV, not the call window. If they cannot, record device as laptop or monitor.

## 3. Condition order (Latin square)

All six orderings of three conditions are used, so every condition appears in every position twice across six participants. Assign in booking order. Participants 7 and 8 restart at orderings 1 and 2.

| Ordering | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | B | C | P1, P7 |
| 2 | B | C | A | P2, P8 |
| 3 | C | A | B | P3 |
| 4 | A | C | B | P4 |
| 5 | B | A | C | P5 |
| 6 | C | B | A | P6 |

For the pilot with n = 3 to 5, use orderings 1 to 5 in order. Record the ordering number on the scoring sheet.

## 4. Per-condition procedure (9 min each)

Say: "Here is Style [1/2/3]. Watch it once as you normally would. Then I will ask a few questions."

Play the clip. Do not pause. Do not answer questions during playback.

**Comprehension questions** (ask in this order; record the answer and mark correct, wrong, or don't know):

1. "There was a line where it was not obvious who said it: [quote the ambiguous line]. Who said it?"
2. "Two people spoke at the same time near [moment]. Who started first?"
3. "There was a sound near [moment]. Where did it come from: left, right, behind, or you could not tell?"
4. "One person spoke while not on screen. Did the captions say who it was, or was it something generic like 'man' or 'voice'?"
5. "What object or action did you see at [moment]?" (This checks whether people were watching the picture, not only the captions.)

Do not confirm or correct answers until the session is over.

**Ratings.** Say: "Now four quick ratings, 1 to 5."

- Readability: "How easy were the captions to read? 1 is very hard, 5 is very easy."
- Distraction: "How much did the captions pull your eyes away from the scene? 1 is not at all, 5 is all the time."
- Comfort: "How comfortable was it to watch for that length? 1 is uncomfortable, 5 is very comfortable."
- Control: "How much did you feel you could tell who was speaking without effort? 1 is not at all, 5 is completely."

**Open questions** (2 min, pick the ones that fit the style; skip for Standard where not relevant):

- "Did the speaker labels feel useful, or repetitive?"
- "The captions sometimes sat on the left or right instead of the bottom. Did that help you find the speaker, or did it make you search for the caption?"
- "Did the scene feel like a normal TV scene, or like it was built to show off the captions?"
- "Anything you would change?"

Write down phrases in the participant's own words.

## 5. Preference ranking and final questions (5 min)

Show the three styles side by side as stills (same frame, all three). Say: "Please rank them: which would you want on your own TV, first, second, third?"

Then ask:

- "Why that first choice?"
- "Is there a show or situation where you would switch styles?"
- "If this existed on your TV, would you turn it on? For what kind of programme?"
- "Was any style hard to read at any point? Which moment?"

## 6. Close (4 min)

Say: "That is everything. Thank you. You will be paid [amount] by [method] within 48 hours. If you think of anything later, message me. You can ask me to delete your data any time before the end of December."

Reveal the correct answers only if asked. Confirm the payment details. End the recording.

## Pilot continuation and kill signals

Decide after the third pilot session and again after the last one. Write the decision and the numbers in the study log.

**Continue when all of these hold:**

- Attribution accuracy is higher in Speaker-aware (B) than Standard (A) across participants.
- At least 2 of the first 3 participants (preferably 4 of 5) rank an enhanced mode (B or C) first for at least one micro-scene.
- Median distraction for B is not worse than for A.
- No severe readability issue in B or C is left unresolved (a severe issue is one the participant says stopped them reading a line).
- Participants describe a real situation where they would use it, in their own words, without prompting from the moderator.

**Kill (or redesign before the final study) when any of these hold:**

- Participants consistently rank Standard first.
- Speaker-aware does not improve attribution over Standard.
- Median distraction for Speaker-aware is worse than Standard.
- Labels or lanes add confusion (participants say they searched for captions, misread the label as dialogue, or report the labels as noise).
- The benefit shows only in the contrived blocking (people say the scene felt engineered and the attribution gain vanishes on the more natural micro-scene).
- The value cannot be explained to a participant without describing how the system works. If the moderator has to explain the architecture for the person to see the point, the point is not there.

If the pilot is mixed (for example attribution improves but distraction is worse), fix the specific cause, re-run two pilot participants, then decide. Do not proceed to the polished scene on a mixed result.
